# Ethics and Rules

Cybersecurity gives us the ability to understand, test, and sometimes break the systems around us. With great power, comes great responsibility...

Anyway....By participating in Cyber Bucs activities, labs, competitions, projects, or other club resources, you are expected to follow these rules and use what you learn responsibly.

## The Cyber Bucs Pledge

As a member of Cyber Bucs, I promise to be a good hacker.

I will use my knowledge to learn, create, protect, and help others. I will respect people's privacy, property, accounts, systems, and data.

I understand that knowing how to attack a system does not give me permission to do so.

I will:

- Only test systems that I own or have explicit permission to test.
- Stay within the defined scope of labs, CTFs, competitions, and authorized security testing.
- Respect the privacy and data of others.
- Follow the rules of competitions, platforms, and organizations we participate in.
- Report vulnerabilities responsibly instead of exploiting them for personal gain.
- Never use club resources or knowledge to intentionally harm others.
- Help create an environment where members can learn, experiment, and ask questions safely.

## Permission and Scope

One of the most important rules in cybersecurity is simple:

**If you do not have permission, do not attack it.**

Finding a system on the internet does not give you permission to scan, exploit, access, or disrupt it.

Practice should take place in environments specifically designed or authorized for security testing, such as CTFs, cybersecurity labs, intentionally vulnerable machines, or systems where you have received explicit permission.

When an activity defines a scope, stay within that scope.

## Club Conduct

Cyber Bucs should be a place where beginners and experienced members can learn together.

Share knowledge, help other members, give credit where it is due, and treat others with respect. Do not shame someone for not knowing something. Everyone starts somewhere.

Do not intentionally damage another member's work, interfere with club infrastructure, steal competition flags, sabotage another team, or misuse access provided through the club.

## Responsible Disclosure

If you accidentally discover a real vulnerability while learning or participating in club activities, do not exploit it further, publish sensitive details, or attempt to prove how much access you can obtain.

Stop, document what you discovered without unnecessarily accessing additional information, and report it through the appropriate channel.

## Remember

The difference between cybersecurity research and unauthorized hacking is not just technical skill. **Permission, scope, intent, and responsible behavior matter.**

Be curious.

Break things that you are allowed to break.

Learn how they work.

Use your skills for good.
