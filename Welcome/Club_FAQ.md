# Cyber Bucs Club FAQ

This page answers some of the most common questions new and current members may have about Cyber Bucs.

---

## 1. What is Cyber Bucs?

Cyber Bucs is a student cybersecurity club focused on learning, practicing, and exploring cybersecurity. We provide opportunities for students to build practical skills, participate in competitions, work on projects, and learn from other members.

## 2. Do I need cybersecurity experience to join?

No. Cyber Bucs welcomes students of all skill levels. You can join even if you have never used Linux, participated in a CTF, or taken a cybersecurity course before.

## 3. What do you do at club meetings?

Meetings can include workshops, demonstrations, cybersecurity labs, CTF challenges, competition preparation, guest speakers, projects, and discussions about cybersecurity topics.

## 4. What can I learn in Cyber Bucs?

You can explore many areas of cybersecurity, including:

- Linux and command-line tools
- Networking
- Penetration testing
- Defensive security
- Digital forensics
- Security tools
- CTF techniques

You will also have opportunities to develop teamwork, problem-solving, research, and technical communication skills.

## 5. Do I need to be a Computer Science or Cybersecurity major?

No. Students from other majors and backgrounds are welcome. If you are interested in cybersecurity and want to learn, you can participate.

## 6. Do I need to bring a laptop?

A laptop is highly recommended for hands-on meetings, labs, and competitions. Some activities may require you to install software or connect to cybersecurity training platforms.

## 7. What software should I have installed?

The software you need depends on the activity. Common tools may include Git, a web browser, virtualization software, and a Linux environment.

Check the guides in the `Start_Here` repository for setup instructions and recommendations.

## 8. Do I need to use Kali Linux?

No. Kali Linux is useful for many cybersecurity activities, but it is not required for everything we do. Other Linux distributions, Windows, WSL, and virtual machines may also be used depending on the activity.

## 9. What is a CTF?

CTF stands for Capture the Flag. A CTF is a cybersecurity competition where participants solve technical challenges to find hidden pieces of information called flags.

Challenges can involve areas such as web security, cryptography, networking, digital forensics, reverse engineering, and system exploitation.

## 10. Can beginners participate in competitions?

Yes. Competitions are one of the best ways to learn.

You are not expected to know how to solve every challenge. More experienced members can help newer members understand tools, techniques, and approaches during team-based competitions.

## 11. How do I know about upcoming meetings and events?

Club announcements, meeting information, competitions, workshops, and other updates will be shared through the club's communication channels (i.e. Discord).

Make sure you regularly check the club's announcements so you do not miss upcoming opportunities.

## 12. What is this GitHub organization for?

Our GitHub organization is used to organize club resources, guides, projects, competition information, and other materials.

The `Start_Here` repository is the best place to begin if you are new to the organization.

## 13. Can I contribute to the club's GitHub repositories?

Yes. Members are encouraged to contribute when appropriate.

Before making changes, read the repository's contribution guidelines and Git/GitHub guide. In general, create a separate branch for your work and submit a Pull Request instead of making changes directly to the `main` branch.

## 14. Can I suggest a workshop, project, or competition?

Absolutely. Members are encouraged to suggest topics they want to learn about or activities they would like the club to participate in.

If there is a cybersecurity topic you want to explore, bring it up with the club or club leadership.

## 15. What is the best way to get started?

Start by exploring the `Start_Here` repository and getting your computer ready for club activities.

After that, the best way to learn is to participate. Attend meetings, try the labs, join competitions, ask questions, and work with other members.

You do not need to understand everything immediately. Cybersecurity is a large field, and everyone starts somewhere.
